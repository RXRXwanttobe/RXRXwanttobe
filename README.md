# About me

I hold a B.S. in Biomedical Engineering and am currently a graduate student studying biomedical imaging and image processing. I am developing my programming skills alongside my studies, with an interest in using computational methods to understand both medical images and biological systems.

## Biomedical imaging and image processing

My current studies cover MRI, CT, and X-ray imaging. I am particularly focused on learning how to work with these images through code: understanding the data, implementing image-processing methods, and interpreting the results.

I use Python and MATLAB as I build these foundations. My goal is to become more confident in translating imaging concepts into practical, understandable analysis workflows.

## From medical images to cellular phenotypes

Over time, I plan to extend these image-processing skills to microscopy and cell images. I am interested in measuring cell morphology and using it as a phenotype to study how cells respond to different conditions and treatments.

Image-based phenotypic profiling and drug discovery, including approaches pursued by companies such as Recursion, are directions I would like to explore. This is a longer-term learning and research goal that grows out of my current work in medical imaging.

## Computational molecular research

Alongside my graduate studies, I work in a company where I am learning and applying molecular docking, cheminformatics, and bioinformatics. I also carry out molecular dynamics simulations as part of this work.

My work includes docking and screening small molecules against protein targets, examining protein–ligand binding poses, and assessing ligand properties. I use PyMOL and DataWarrior for visualization and interpretation, and Python and MATLAB for analysis and workflow development.

## What I share here

This GitHub is a place for the computational work that I can share publicly, including:

- Biomedical image-processing exercises, code, and learning notes.
- Structure-based analysis workflows and comparisons of existing research tools.
- Reproducible benchmarks, visualizations, and small utilities developed during my learning.

I aim to document how each workflow works, how its results were evaluated, and where its limitations lie. As I learn more, I hope to connect my interests in biomedical imaging, cellular phenotypes, and computational drug discovery.
